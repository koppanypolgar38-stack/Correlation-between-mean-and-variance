# Correlation-between-mean-and-variance
In this "research" I made a bunch of simulations in Excel, to figure out whether is there any correlation between mean and the variance 
I simulated 700 times from different probability disrtibutions to calculate tha average, and the variance of these numbers. Then simulated this 700-sample 1500 times, so I got 1500 mean, and 1500 variance data, to find out is there any relationship between the mean, and the variance.
First of all, I started with the normal distribution, I also calculated a confidence interval for the average of 30 correlation coeffitients, to check whether 0 is in the interval. These 30 correlation coeffitients or r-values comes from the 1500 sample: I calculated the correlation between average and variance for the first 50 simulations, then I continue whith the next 50... I also calculated the correlation for the 1500-sample. 
At the normal distribution it seems like there is no correlation, this was not interesting, because we know it from the Lukács-theorem: The datas are independent if the background variable is normal. Of course correlation and independence are not the same, but if something is independent that's also uncorrelated.
I tested the uniform distribution, but I had no reason to refuse the 0-hipothesis, so there weren't correlation between the datas
However when I tested the exponential distribution I found a strong correlation between the mean and the variance they were positively correlated, with a value of 0,68. I attempted to define the p-value by simulation, I simulated the datas 20 times, and 0 had never been in the confidence interval, so from this we can conclude that the p-value were very close to 0.
Finally I tested the F-distribution and the situation was very similar to the exponential one. The correlation was around 0,5, with a variance of 0,1 (strongly positive).
These observations ara not a strong "magic thing", because we can derive it from the Lukács-theorem, or at the last two cases: the exponential and the F distributions are right-shifted. So if we put in a bigger number of course the variance will also grow. Or an other reasing: the Expected value of the exponential distribution is: 1/lambda, and the variance is the same
